# Evidence audit for the 11 presentation defects

Snapshot: `codex/visible-density-gate-20261113` at `f3adbc770932c1b23a1efdfa8cc6c571a2ed0a1a` (2026-11-13). This matrix separates implemented hard gates from visual or semantic judgments they cannot prove. A gate is evidence that a particular failure mode is screened; it is not evidence that a deck is beautiful, correct, or suitable for its audience.

| User-reported defect | Formal-path safeguard and evidence | Status and residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Ugly fonts and templates | The selected theme's font families and contrast are checked against emitted PPTX runs; the title must match an emitted text box and remain at least 28 pt. Layout repetition is checked from semantic signatures and emitted object geometry. See `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (font, contrast, title hierarchy, layout diversity) and `docs/TITLE_HIERARCHY_REPORT.md`. | **Partial.** These checks rule out some illegible or repetitive outputs. They cannot rate taste, typography pairing, image treatment, or whether a theme looks polished. Page-by-page design review remains necessary. |
| 2. Nearly empty slides with a few words | The formal plan has a non-lowerable 40-unit explanation floor; repeated copy does not pad the count. Rendered OCR also requires minimum body-text occupancy and at least two lines. See `tests/deck-gate.test.mjs` (non-lowerable minimum) and `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (sparse authored/rendered copy). | **Partial, with hard floors.** A passing minimum does not prove the explanation is useful or that the page uses its space well. Topic-specific sufficiency remains a human content-density review. |
| 3. Overlap and unreadably small type | Emitted OOXML text bounds and overlaps are checked; effective run sizes, including persisted autofit, must be measurable and at least 14 pt; chart text is read from chart XML; title floor is 28 pt. OCR checks body/title overflow. See `tests/layout-audit.test.mjs`, `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs`, and `docs/HORIZONTAL_TEXT_OVERFLOW_REPORT.md`. | **Strong for measured cases, not absolute.** OCR can miss text, and renderer/font substitution can differ by machine. Inspect rendered pages on the target presentation software. |
| 4. No useful images or diagrams | A full deck must materialize visuals on at least half of content pages; planned visuals must exist; visual objects/groups have minimum size and structured diagrams are checked for graph shape. See `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (50% floor, rendered geometry and size) and `tests/presentation-harness.test.mjs` (unmaterialized Mermaid fallback). | **Partial.** It does not establish that an image is relevant, licensed correctly in every context, or explanatory. Human `visual_support` review is still required. |
| 5. Architecture as meaningless boxes and arrows | Architecture validation requires named components, distinct directed action relationships, explicit entry/output, connected paths, and every node on an entry-to-output path. Invalid graphs are rejected before rendering. See `tests/presentation-harness.test.mjs` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE_LOGIC_GATE_REPORT.md`. | **Structural safeguard only.** Valid topology does not prove the represented system or causal explanation is true. Human `diagram_logic` review remains required. |
| 6. No agenda, sections, or narrative organization | Full-deck plans require an agenda, a titled transition for each section, assigned content, per-slide roles and a page map; the approved page map is bound to rendered headlines. See `tests/deck-gate.test.mjs` and `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (agenda, transition, assignment and roadmap). | **Enforced for declared structure.** The system cannot prove the chosen outline is the right story or that transitions communicate the intended logic. |
| 7. Assistant-to-user language in the presentation | Pattern checks inspect visible slide text and speaker notes for common direct-address and conversational prompts. See `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (English/Chinese voice and notes) and `tests/presentation-harness.test.mjs` (formal-path regression). | **Partial.** Novel paraphrases can escape pattern checks, and ordinary research narration must remain allowed. Human `presentation_voice` review is required. |
| 8. Opaque Chinese or long English passages in Chinese decks | Language checks block substantial English-dominant blocks and aggregate multiple shorter fragments; reviewer records include `language_clarity`. See `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (single and split English blocks). | **Partial.** Readability, jargon, awkward translation and the need for a technical English term require a fluent reader's judgment. |
| 9. Same template repeated throughout | Full-deck checks detect repeated composition signatures, low signature variety, dominant signatures and repeated measured object geometry even if plan labels differ. See `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (alternating and geometry-only repeated templates). | **Partial.** It detects geometry/composition repetition, not every repeated color, typography or illustration style; deliberate recurring grids may also be appropriate. Human `layout_variety` review remains required. |
| 10. Tiny clutter and defensive caveats everywhere | The 14 pt text floor covers measured visible OOXML runs; caveat checks block stacked, repeated and deck-wide disclaimer-style clauses. See `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (small text and caveat overload). | **Partial.** Phrase matching cannot decide whether an uncommon disclaimer is useful, and it does not identify every kind of irrelevant microcopy. The reviewer checks caveat relevance. |
| 11. Wrong allocation of depth and detail | Plans declare topic depth; standard priorities require at least two core pages and deep priorities at least seven. The formal audit checks page/time shares and distinct explanation stages, and rejects repeated filler across priority pages. See `tests/deck-gate.test.mjs` and `tests/quality-audit.test.mjs` (priority depth, stage and time allocation). | **Partial.** The plan can mislabel importance or depth. Page counts and talk time do not prove the central mechanism is explained well or that supporting detail is genuinely unimportant. Human `emphasis_depth` review remains required. |

## What the evidence supports

The repository has deterministic, adversarial regression coverage for many observable failure modes in the formal Harness. The last verified full suite at this snapshot reported 364 passing, 11 skipped optional-dependency checks, and zero failures. A seven-page synthetic PPTX was also rendered with PowerPoint 16.0 and Poppler and reviewed page by page during the title-hierarchy phase; that synthetic artifact is not evidence of quality for arbitrary user decks.

No current evidence supports a claim that all 11 defects are completely solved or that every generated deck will be beautiful. The strongest remaining gap is the distinction between **structural compliance** and **semantic/design quality**: the system can enforce measurable floors and require a page-specific human review record, but it cannot authenticate the reviewer or independently prove that a visual is relevant, an explanation is clear, or the narrative gives the right topic the right depth.

## Next implementation target

Strengthen the reviewer gate's evidence quality without treating a self-reported `pass` as independent verification: require the page review record to cite concrete emitted page evidence for every applicable dimension, and add adversarial tests showing that missing, generic, or stale evidence cannot approve a deck. Keep human judgment for taste and meaning, while making the proof of that judgment specific to the rendered artifact hash.
