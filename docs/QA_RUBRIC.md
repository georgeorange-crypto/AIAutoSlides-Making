# QA Rubric

Apply to every generated variant; report `pass`, `fail`, `blocked`, or `not-run` with evidence paths. Structural checks and visual review are separate.

1. **Provenance and facts:** all factual slides map valid source IDs and locators; unknown IDs fail; unverified evidence remains visibly unverified; plotted values equal deterministic source transforms; no generated claim is presented as verified.
2. **Rights and privacy:** each nontrivial asset has source, creator, license/permission and review state; private inputs remain local unless explicitly authorized; no paid request without configuration and approval.
3. **Structure and editability:** record slide count; text/shape/chart/image counts; native chart workbook/value comparison; notes; editable level per output/element; raster fallback proportion. File extension alone proves nothing.
4. **Visual quality:** render all pages at fixed size/font environment; inspect every page for clipping, overlap, contrast, label readability, font substitution, hierarchy and consistency. Store renderer/version and page images. Automated geometry does not replace visual review.
   The structural QA also reads each emitted PPTX text run's explicit RGB color and checks a 4.5:1 contrast floor against the slide's solid background and known solid fills beneath the text. Simple alpha fills are composited over the known slide background. An unrecognized/inherited text color or an image, gradient, transformed, or otherwise unsupported background is reported as unmeasured and blocks formal QA; it is never treated as passing. This check does not measure rendered pixels, complex layer blending, charts' internal labels, or font appearance. See `docs/PPTX_TEXT_CONTRAST_REPORT.md`.
   Chart-part text styles are also inspected directly: data labels, axes, and legends must expose a measurable font and size. Missing chart styles or broken slide-to-chart relationships fail closed; formal QA blocks chart text below 14 pt, outside the selected theme's heading/body fonts, or unmeasurable. Rendered OCR checks chart-region word-box intersections and title words against authored title geometry; positive and unmeasurable results block. Thresholds and OCR limits are documented in `docs/CHART_TYPOGRAPHY_REPORT.md` and `docs/HORIZONTAL_TEXT_OVERFLOW_REPORT.md`. These checks do not certify category spacing or overall readability, so visual inspection remains required.
5. **Narrative/accessibility:** one main assertion per factual slide, concise support, readable type, meaningful chart labels/units, chart description and usable speaker notes; check requested duration against supplied script and rate. A formal Harness deck plan must specify audience, purpose, language, style, ordered content priorities, sections and prohibited copy patterns; its first slide or second after an optional cover must be an agenda listing every section, each section must have one titled transition before its content, and every content slide must map to a declared section. Each content slide lists the declared priorities it develops in `priority_ids`; each declared priority must have at least one core/high page owner, and every core/high page must map to a declared priority. Each slide must declare role, importance, explanation goal, visual rationale and layout intent.
6. **Reproducibility:** record tool/runtime versions, configuration hash, inputs/outputs and SHA-256, transformation, warnings, duration, retries and cost. Re-run with same inputs and compare deterministic outputs where supported.
7. **Repair:** findings identify `slide_id` and `element_id`; only impacted artifacts are rebuilt; regression-check unaffected pages and retain before/after evidence.

No aggregate score may conceal a failed hard constraint. Missing external executable or authorized account is `blocked`, not pass.

## Title hierarchy

Every slide title must match an emitted PPTX text box and have an effective font size of at least 28 pt after any persisted PowerPoint `normAutofit` scaling. A missing title match or unmeasurable size blocks formal approval. This enforces a clear headline size floor; it does not prove that the title is well worded, appropriately concise, or visually attractive.

The full-deck layout-diversity gate checks both semantic composition signatures and measured object geometry from the emitted PPTX. It blocks when one body-object arrangement appears on at least 60% of all content pages and geometry is measurable on at least three pages, even if the IR assigns different slide types or element kinds. This narrows a template-relabeling loophole; it does not rate aesthetics or distinguish an intentional, useful recurring grid from monotonous reuse. Review every rendered page for actual visual variety.

## Architecture diagram logic

The formal validator rejects architecture diagrams with fewer than three named components, fewer than two directed relationships, repeated source-to-target arrows, no explicit entry or output component, disconnected components, or nodes outside an entry-to-output path. Edge labels must use a recognized action relationship. These are structural plausibility checks: they cannot establish that the arrows match the real system, that labels describe the right mechanism, or that the chosen abstraction answers the audience's question. Human `diagram_logic` review remains required.

Formal plans require at least 40 visible explanation units per content page; a Han character or a non-Han word counts as one unit. The rendered-density gate separately requires at least two visible body-text lines and a minimum occupancy ratio. Agenda, section-transition, cover, and ending roles use their own structure checks and are not forced to meet the content-page floor.

## Formal Harness page review

Formal deck completion requires a human reviewer to record a note for every page under each of these checks: content density, typography, visual support, diagram logic, narrative role, presentation voice, language clarity, layout variety, caveat relevance, emphasis/depth allocation, and rendered fit. Each page review must cite the SHA-256 of the exact rendered page image recorded by the Harness; approval re-hashes the stored PNG and blocks missing, changed, or stale page images. `not_applicable` requires a reason. Missing checks, unresolved findings, notes shorter than 12 informative text units (Han characters or Latin words), or repeated notes across quality dimensions on the same page block approval. Reviewers must describe a page-specific observation for each dimension; this rejects empty or copied boilerplate, but it cannot authenticate the reviewer or prove the observation is honest. These records are reviewer attestations bound to the rendered deck and page-image hashes; they do not prove reviewer identity, that a human actually opened the cited image, taste, or truthfulness, and automated geometry/OCR still cannot certify aesthetic quality or narrative adequacy.

The formal Harness path also runs a deterministic hard gate against the actual PPTX QA manifest and rendered pages. A version 1.2.0 plan must declare talk duration, recognized slide importance levels, and minimum core/high page and speaking-time shares (at least 25% and 50%, respectively). Every substantive slide must cite at least one source/evidence ID, and the approved page map's IDs must be carried into the corresponding Presentation IR slide; IDs must exist in the supplied source/evidence ledger. On the full-deck run it blocks a missing or incomplete agenda, missing or misnamed section transitions, unassigned content slides, section-transition copy with fewer than 18 visible text units beyond its heading, text outside bounds, any detected text-box overlap, authored text whose effective size is below 14 pt even in the footer area, after applying any persisted OOXML `normAutofit fontScale`, any visible text run whose size cannot be measured from its run or paragraph-default OOXML style, including native table cells, authored text with no measurable font family, and text fonts outside the selected theme's declared heading/body pair. It also blocks missing page images, visible content below the planned minimum explanation length, body-text occupancy below 5.5% on visual pages or 7% on text-only pages, fewer than two rendered body-text lines, two or more OCR-detected authored body words outside their matched text-box bounds on any side (with a 1.5% page-size tolerance), materialized-visual coverage below the greater of the plan threshold or the enforced 50% floor, image elements below 8% slide area, chart/table elements below 15%, structured diagram/process/card object-box coverage below its type-specific floor or overall footprint below 12%, a planned visual missing from its page, recognized assistant-to-user phrasing in visible copy or speaker notes, English-dominant blocks in a Chinese deck (one text block with at least 16 Latin words and under 35% Han characters among Han and Latin letters, or multiple English-dominant fragments with at least 4 Latin words each and at least 16 total under the same Han-share threshold), five consecutive content pages with the same composition signature, fewer than 40% distinct composition signatures across five or more content pages, one composition signature covering at least 60% of content pages whenever a full deck has at least three content pages, three or more disclaimer-style clauses on one non-agenda page, the same disclaimer repeated on two or more non-agenda pages (including cover, section-transition, and ending pages), or distinct disclaimer-style caveats appearing on at least three non-agenda pages and at least 40% of those pages, core/high pages below the declared share, and core/high talk time below the declared share. Layout signatures use slide type, structured element kinds, and coarse rendered body-object distribution when available; they ignore plan-authored `layout_intent`. Representative-slide pilots defer full-deck visual-coverage and diversity checks; a pilot page may not represent the complete deck. The voice screen also blocks common audience-conversation cues such as “let’s take a look”, “as you can see”, “我们一起来看”, and “带你走进”, alongside direct-address phrases; it preserves ordinary research narration such as “we demonstrate” and “我们证明”; it is a pattern screen, so unusual paraphrases may escape it and an unfortunate phrase may need editorial review. When slide durations are estimated, recognized importance levels adjust the allocation; authored durations remain unchanged. A single specific caveat remains allowed. The caveat phrase screen catches common English and Chinese forms; it cannot decide whether one caveat is relevant or whether differently worded caveats repeat the same idea. OCR failures are reported as warnings for human inspection rather than treated as proof of clipping. The font-family check compares PPTX-authored run fonts with the bundled theme declaration; it cannot confirm that every recipient device has those fonts or prevent PowerPoint from substituting a missing system font. The gate cannot choose the right importance labels or establish that the core pages explain a topic well; those decisions remain in the approved plan and human `emphasis_depth` review.

Before rendering, the approved page-map `assertion` is also compared with each included IR page's headline (`assertion_title`, otherwise `title`); a missing or different headline blocks the run. This prevents approved outline text from drifting away from the generated slide heading, but cannot determine whether that heading is informative or factually sound.


For every declared content priority, plan schema 1.2.0 requires an approved `priority_depth` declaration and distinctive `priority_terms`. The full-deck audit matches complete terms against visible body elements on each mapped core/high page; title/assertion-only mentions do not count, and English terms must match whole word/phrase boundaries. A page matching none is blocked and per-page matches are reported. This catches unrelated tags, title-only name-dropping, and English substring collisions, but body copy can still name-drop a term and terms can be chosen poorly. The formal gate also checks distinct core/high page allocations: standard depth requires at least two pages; deep depth requires at least seven. Deep priorities must label their core pages with `explanation_stage` and cover at least four distinct stages. It blocks substantial exact sentences or bullets repeated within one content page, counts repeated copy only once toward that page explanation floor, and blocks identical or near-verbatim substantial text reused across core/high pages for the same priority. These checks cannot establish that each page substantively performs its stage, that the plan chose the right priorities, or that the pages form a coherent and factually correct explanation. Those judgments remain in the approved plan and human page-by-page `emphasis_depth` review.
